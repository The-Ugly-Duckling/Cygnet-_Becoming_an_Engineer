# Cygnet-_Becoming_an_Engineer
Not yet a swan: A public journal of growth, documenting the lifelong journey from student to engineer through learning, reflection, craftsmanship, and service.
Cygnet: Becoming an Engineer

Greetings to all.

Before I begin, I remember the Almighty, the omnipresent One who has sustained us for so long. I bow my head in gratitude and seek forgiveness for all the mistakes and sins I have committed, knowingly and unknowingly, in the past and present, in this life and in all that came before it. O Divine One, guide me in writing and completing this work so that whoever reads it, sees it, or follows it may be blessed and benefited.

Now, I shall begin.

I cordially invite all kind, hardworking, and honest software developers and engineers to witness my journey toward becoming a true engineer.
At this moment, I consider myself an infant in the realm of programming and engineering. To me, being a developer and being an engineer are two different things. A developer may be someone capable of writing, compiling, and running code. An engineer, however, is someone who has deeply understood the principles behind the code, studied the concepts with dedication, transformed knowledge into wisdom, and applied that wisdom to create meaningful technology.
Technology is applied knowledge. Engineering is applied understanding.

I am writing this journal to challenge myself to solve concepts on my own and to document my growth. This journey is mine alone to walk. You must play your own game; I will play mine.

My Views on Computer Science

Computer Science is a fascinating discipline. It gives us the freedom to learn, understand, and explore the machine we call a computer.
Artificial Intelligence is perhaps the most discussed and misunderstood technology of our time. Many say it can be either beneficial or harmful. I believe that the technology itself is neither. AI is like fire: it can be used to destroy, or it can be used to cook food and sustain life. The responsibility lies with us. Our task is not to fear technology but to learn how to use it wisely, ethically, and for the benefit of humanity.

On Education and Learning

I believe children should first learn how to be human before they learn how to program.
Before teaching programming, we should teach kindness, communication, curiosity, discipline, critical thinking, and a scientific temperament. Programming is a tool, and one should first understand what tools are meant to accomplish. We should raise thoughtful and responsible human beings first, and engineers second.

When engineers become mere machines that only follow instructions, they become replaceable. In such a world, Artificial Intelligence becomes a threat. But an engineer who can think, question, create, and innovate will always remain valuable.

Develop logic, not just vocabulary. Develop understanding, not memorization.

Respect for Every Discipline
I have chosen Computer Science and Engineering, but that does not make other fields any less important.
Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, Electronic, Agricultural, Medical, and countless other professionals contribute to society in ways that make our lives possible. Every discipline has dignity and purpose. We should respect all forms of honest work and recognize the interconnected nature of human progress.

Responsibility Toward Society

We earn our livelihoods from society, and therefore we owe a debt back to society.
Society is not only the people around us. It also includes the animals, plants, rivers, soil, air, and every biotic and abiotic component that supports life.

Whenever possible:
Grow some of your own food.
Conserve water.
Save seeds.
Help protect the environment.
Learn to transform waste into something useful.

Small actions, when multiplied by millions of people, create meaningful change.

On Family and Education

Try to teach your children, younger siblings, and family members whenever you can. Encourage independence rather than dependency.
Modern education, coaching culture, and many institutions have flaws. Instead of complaining, we should contribute to their improvement.
Encourage children to read books. Let books remain the primary source of learning, with Artificial Intelligence used as a supporting tool rather than a replacement for thought. Use AI to assist understanding, not to avoid thinking.
Books first. Reasoning always. AI when necessary.


A Final Message
Stay happy.
Stay kind.
To those who have lost opportunities or jobs because of technological change, know that you are not alone. Humanity has faced many transformations before, and we can adapt once again. The answer is not to fight technology blindly but to guide it responsibly.
Do not live like a helpless follower. Be human. Think. Question. Improve. Create.
Let us build technology that remains humane, ethical, and beneficial for all.
As I begin my journey toward becoming an engineer, I hope to grow not only in knowledge but also in wisdom, character, and service to others.

If my words resonate with you, you may always reach out to me through this medium. Together, we may learn, collaborate, and perhaps even create something better than what exists today. No single individual can perfect a system alone, but through honest discussion, shared effort, and a genuine desire to improve, we can contribute toward a future that is more humane, thoughtful, and beneficial for everyone.

This is only the beginning of my journey. I invite you to walk your own path with courage, curiosity, and kindness.
